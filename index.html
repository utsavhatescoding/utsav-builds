Behind TradePulseThe question, the approach, the product +
The question

What if public data were easier to use?

Customs releases contain valuable information. Finding the relevant product, country or monthly change should not require working through an entire workbook.

The approach

Make the information legible.

Bring the data, visual exploration and written analysis together, so people can move from a headline to the detail behind it.

The product

Two ways to explore.

A website for reports and data downloads, plus a dedicated dashboard for exploring Nepal’s foreign trade.

02 / The person behind the projects

Economics is the lens.
Building is
the practice.

I’m Utsav, an economics and business researcher with a habit of turning questions into projects.

I’m interested in what happens when research leaves the page: a dataset becomes a useful tool, a market shift becomes a clear story, or local knowledge becomes a better way to travel.

Nepal is my starting point. I want to build things that understand the local context and are thoughtful enough to stand on their own anywhere.

Always up for a good conversation ↗
My working philosophyA simple process. A lot of questions.
01

Start with a question.

Understand the people, the context and the information before deciding what to build.

02

Make the complex clear.

Find the useful signal. Give it a form that someone can understand and act on.

03

Put it into the world.

Build something people can try. Keep learning from how they actually use it.

On my mind / open questions

01

Who is public data really for?

+

I’m interested in the gap between publishing information and making it useful. What would change if a small business could explore economic data as easily as a researcher?

02

What does local knowledge unlock?

+

A place is more than a list of attractions. I’m exploring how digital products can help people discover it through the guides, communities and everyday journeys that give it meaning.

03

Can small products have a big standard?

+

I think independent projects from Nepal deserve the same attention to clarity, usefulness and craft as products built anywhere else. That’s the standard I’m working towards.
